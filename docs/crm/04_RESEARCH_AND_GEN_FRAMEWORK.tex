\section{ClientOpsCRM Research and Generative Engineering
Framework}\label{clientopscrm-research-and-generative-engineering-framework}

\subsection{Research objective}\label{research-objective}

The goal was not to clone a vendor screen or reproduce a fashionable
feature list. The research question was: which CRM primitives remain
structurally important across enterprise products, smaller sales CRMs,
academic CRM theory, data-system engineering and secure software
practice, and how can those primitives be implemented with deterministic
local behavior on top of Relay\textquotesingle s existing intake core?

The release research corpus contains 143 unique URLs: 77 inherited from
Relay 1.2.1 and 66 added for this CRM work. It includes first-party
product documentation, database/security standards, peer-reviewed CRM
and decision-science work, and an official Nobel Prize source. The
canonical list is \texttt{research/research\_sources.csv}; design claims
are mapped in \texttt{research/CLAIM\_SOURCE\_LEDGER.csv}.

\subsection{Cross-source convergence}\label{cross-source-convergence}

\subsubsection{CRM is a process system, not a contact
list}\label{crm-is-a-process-system-not-a-contact-list}

Payne and Frow\textquotesingle s strategic framework treats CRM as
cross-functional processes spanning strategy, value creation,
multichannel integration, information management and performance
assessment. Reinartz, Krafft and Hoyer operationalize CRM as a
measurable process rather than a single software feature. Kumar and
Reinartz similarly separate strategic, analytical and operational CRM.
This argues for a small coherent state machine with measurable
transitions rather than isolated address-book CRUD.

\subsubsection{Lead and opportunity are different
states}\label{lead-and-opportunity-are-different-states}

Dynamics 365, Pipedrive, Odoo and SAP all distinguish earlier
lead/qualification work from opportunities/deals that enter a sales
cycle or pipeline. The release therefore does not put every intake
record directly into the opportunity pipeline. \texttt{lead\ qualify} is
explicit and \texttt{lead\ convert} is transactional.

\subsubsection{Relationships need durable entity
identity}\label{relationships-need-durable-entity-identity}

HubSpot\textquotesingle s current contacts/companies/deals APIs
emphasize separate objects linked by associations and unique
identifiers. Dynamics models accounts and contacts separately; Pipedrive
distinguishes people and organizations. ClientOpsCRM mirrors the durable
concept, not vendor schemas: accounts and contacts are independent
records, and deals link to them without embedding copies as the only
source of truth.

\subsubsection{Pipeline state must be
explicit}\label{pipeline-state-must-be-explicit}

Pipedrive documents stages as steps through a real sales process and
uses stage/deal probability for pipeline management. Odoo and SAP use
stage/probability/value for pipeline analysis and forecasting.
ClientOpsCRM stores the pipeline, stage, stage kind and probability
explicitly and checks their consistency below the command layer.

\subsubsection{Activities are first-class
history}\label{activities-are-first-class-history}

Pipedrive, Odoo and SAP all associate calls/tasks/meetings/follow-up
activities with CRM objects. ClientOpsCRM supplies tasks plus a generic
business \texttt{activities} history so state changes do not disappear
into the current record snapshot.

\subsubsection{Audit is different from activity
history}\label{audit-is-different-from-activity-history}

Microsoft Dataverse auditing exists to answer who changed what and when.
This is a different purpose from the sales timeline. ClientOpsCRM
therefore has both \texttt{activities} and \texttt{audit\_log}; audit is
guarded against update/delete in SQLite.

\subsubsection{Search, custom fields and import are core extensibility
surfaces}\label{search-custom-fields-and-import-are-core-extensibility-surfaces}

HubSpot has dedicated search, properties and imports APIs; Pipedrive
provides data/custom fields and spreadsheet import. ClientOpsCRM makes
FTS search, custom values and CSV/JSON interchange first-class rather
than treating them as later decoration.

\subsubsection{Forecasts should expose
assumptions}\label{forecasts-should-expose-assumptions}

SAP/Odoo/Pipedrive all connect opportunity values, probability/stage and
expected closing to forecasts or pipeline analysis. Decision-science
research by Tversky and Kahneman warns that judgments under uncertainty
are framing-sensitive. ClientOpsCRM therefore deliberately labels its
forecast as deterministic weighted pipeline arithmetic, not an objective
probability model or AI prediction. The operator can inspect every value
and stage probability used.

\subsection{Engineering framework
used}\label{engineering-framework-used}

The implementation loop is evidence-driven:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Observe} --- inspect the real Relay files and executable
  behavior.
\item
  \textbf{Extract invariants} --- identify durable concepts repeated
  across credible sources.
\item
  \textbf{Specify failure before success} --- define invalid
  transitions, duplicate identities, malformed data, partial-write
  hazards and audit tampering before coding happy paths.
\item
  \textbf{Implement at two layers} --- command validation plus database
  constraints/triggers for invariants that must survive direct SQL
  access.
\item
  \textbf{Construct inverse/adversarial tests} --- attempt precisely the
  mutation or partial state the invariant is meant to reject.
\item
  \textbf{Run build parity} --- release/debug/sanitizer builds use the
  same source and warning policy.
\item
  \textbf{Preserve provenance} --- actor, source lead, Relay
  UUID/metadata, activities and audit survive transformations.
\item
  \textbf{Package only proven artifacts} --- generated release bytes are
  hashed, enumerated and ZIP-tested.
\end{enumerate}

This is the project\textquotesingle s ``new-gen'' framework: novelty is
not an unsupported feature. Novelty is produced by recombining proven
CRM process structure with Relay\textquotesingle s deterministic intake,
transactional local persistence, executable invariants, and adversarial
verification so the resulting system has a smaller trust surface than a
typical multi-service CRM stack.

\subsection{Design decisions that were
rejected}\label{design-decisions-that-were-rejected}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Copying vendor schemas or branding: unnecessary and would reduce
  independence.
\item
  Treating all Relay requests as deals: destroys the qualification
  boundary.
\item
  Floating-point money: avoidable error source.
\item
  Free-form deal status independent of stage: permits contradictory
  states.
\item
  Mutable audit records: defeats the purpose of local audit evidence.
\item
  Regex-heavy validation solely for appearance: deterministic parsers
  are easier to audit and avoided a compiler/sanitizer false-positive
  encountered during strict builds.
\item
  Silently replacing an unknown schema: risks corruption/downgrade.
\item
  Generating an AI forecast without a validated training/evaluation
  dataset: would be a mock prediction rather than evidence-backed
  functionality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research stop rule}\label{research-stop-rule}

Research stopped after the core design claims were independently
supported across multiple product families or primary technical
standards, academic CRM sources supplied the process-level model, the
added corpus exceeded the requested 100-source gate, and additional
vendor pages were no longer changing the entity/state/integrity design.
The retained corpus remains available for future feature work.
